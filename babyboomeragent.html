Medicare has four parts. That is the whole puzzle.

Once you know what each letter does, the mail stops being frightening.

A

Hospital

Inpatient stays, skilled nursing, hospice. Most people have already paid for this through working years.

B

Doctors

Office visits, outpatient care, tests and equipment. This one has a monthly premium.

C

Advantage

A private plan that bundles A and B, often with drug coverage. A different way to get the same benefits.

D

Prescriptions

Drug coverage. Skipping it when you first qualify is where most late penalties come from.

Your enrollment window

Seven months around your 65th birthday. Then once a year after that.

  1. 3 months before

    Window opens. Coverage can start on time.

  2. 2 months before

    Best time to compare, before the rush.

  3. Birthday month

    Coverage may be delayed if you wait this long.

  4. 3 months after

    Window closes. After this it gets expensive.

  5. Oct 15 – Dec 7

    Every year after: change plans for January.

The late penalty is permanent. Enroll after your window without other coverage and the surcharge is added to your premium for as long as you have Medicare. It is the single most expensive mistake on this page, and it is completely avoidable.

Questions people ask Chris

Do I have to take Medicare at 65 if I am still working?
Not always. If you have creditable coverage through an employer, you may be able to delay without a penalty. Whether yours counts is the part worth asking about, because getting it wrong is expensive.
What is the difference between Advantage and a Supplement?
Two different ways to handle what Medicare does not pay. One bundles everything into a private plan with a network. The other sits alongside Original Medicare and covers the gaps. Which one fits depends on your doctors, your prescriptions, and how much you travel.
Will my prescriptions be covered?
That depends on the plan's formulary, and formularies change every year. This is the single most common reason a plan that worked last year stops working this year.
Does it cost me anything to work with Chris?
No. Independent agents are paid by the plan you enroll in, at the same rate no matter which one you choose. Your premium is the same whether you use an agent or not.
What if I already picked a plan and I am not happy?
You are not stuck forever. There is an annual window every fall, and certain life changes open a window of their own. Worth a phone call to find out which one applies to you.
